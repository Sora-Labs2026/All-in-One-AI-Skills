# Customs and trade compliance: classification, valuation, origin, Incoterms, duties and screening

Read this when goods cross a border: classifying products, preparing commercial invoices, choosing Incoterms, claiming preferential origin, estimating duties, or screening customers and partners against restricted-party lists. **This is general information, not customs, tax or legal advice.** Customs law is national, changes often and is applied to specific facts; the importer of record, a licensed customs broker and trade counsel make the decisions. Write their questions down; do not answer them with certainty.

## Ground rules

- **Never classify from a product name.** Get the technical facts: materials and their shares by weight and value, function, how it works, how it is packed and sold, and its intended use.
- **Cite the source for every rate and rule** (tariff schedule, ruling, agreement text) with the date you checked it. Duty rates, trade remedies and sanctions change without warning.
- **State uncertainty.** "Our analysis points to heading 8471; a binding ruling would settle it" is honest. "This is classified as 8471" is not your call.
- **Screening hits stop the transaction** until a qualified person clears them. Never clear a match because the name "looks different".
- **Keep the paper trail.** Retention duties run for years (for example five years for US entry records; at least three years under the EU Union Customs Code, longer in some member states).

## Tariff classification (Harmonized System)

The Harmonized System (HS) is a six-digit nomenclature maintained by the World Customs Organization: two digits for the chapter, four for the heading, six for the subheading. The HS 2022 edition is in force; the next edition is scheduled for 1 January 2028. Countries add digits: the US Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTSUS) uses 10 digits for imports and Schedule B for exports; the EU uses the 8-digit Combined Nomenclature and 10-digit TARIC codes; the UK uses 10-digit commodity codes for imports.

Apply the General Rules of Interpretation (GRI) strictly in order, moving on only when the earlier rule does not decide:

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| GRI 1 | Classification follows the terms of the headings and the section and chapter notes. Most goods are settled here; read every relevant note first |
| GRI 2(a) | Incomplete, unfinished or unassembled goods that have the essential character of the finished article classify as that article |
| GRI 2(b) | Mixtures and combinations of materials are taken to the heading for the material, then resolved by GRI 3 |
| GRI 3(a) | The most specific description wins |
| GRI 3(b) | Mixtures, composite goods and retail sets classify by the component giving essential character (judged by nature, bulk, quantity, weight, value or role in use) |
| GRI 3(c) | Otherwise, the heading that occurs last in numerical order among those equally deserving |
| GRI 4 | Goods still unclassifiable go to the heading for the most akin goods |
| GRI 5 | Cases and packing materials: when they classify with the goods and when separately |
| GRI 6 | Apply the same logic at subheading level, comparing only subheadings at the same level, with subheading notes |

Workflow:

1. Write the product description from the technical facts.
2. Find candidate headings, read section and chapter notes (exclusions often decide), apply GRI 1, then 2 to 6 only if needed.
3. Check the WCO Explanatory Notes (or national equivalents) and existing rulings for similar goods: US Customs and Border Protection's rulings database (CROSS), EU Binding Tariff Information (BTI) decisions, UK Advance Tariff Rulings. Rulings for other parties are persuasive, not binding on you.
4. Record the rationale: headings considered, notes applied, rule that decided and why. This record is the defence in an audit.
5. For important or disputed products, suggest the owner request a binding ruling.

Frequent traps: multifunction machines (principal function, and section notes for machines), parts versus accessories (Section XVI and XVII notes), textiles (fibre by weight), food preparations versus raw ingredients, and kits put up for retail sale versus separate items.

## Customs value

Most countries follow the WTO Customs Valuation Agreement. Methods apply in order: (1) transaction value of the goods; (2) transaction value of identical goods; (3) of similar goods; (4) deductive value from resale in the import country; (5) computed value from production costs (4 and 5 may be swapped at the importer's request in many regimes); (6) a reasonable fallback that is not arbitrary.

Transaction value is the price paid or payable, adjusted:

- **Add** (when not already in the price): assists supplied free or cheaply to the producer (moulds, tooling, designs), royalties and licence fees paid as a condition of sale, selling commissions, packing, and proceeds of resale that go back to the seller.
- **Freight and insurance** depend on the country: the US values on a free-on-board basis (international freight excluded); the EU and UK include transport and insurance to the point of entry.
- **Related parties:** the relationship must not have influenced the price; transfer-pricing adjustments after import may require a reconciliation or amendment procedure. Customs and income-tax transfer pricing look at the same transaction differently.

Incoterms set who pays what; they do not change the valuation rules of the importing country.

## Origin and trade agreements

- **Non-preferential origin** (country of origin marking, trade remedies, quotas) uses "wholly obtained" or "last substantial transformation" tests.
- **Preferential origin** under a trade agreement uses that agreement's product-specific rules: a change in tariff classification of non-originating inputs, a regional value content threshold, a specific process, or combinations. Look up the rule for the product's heading in the agreement's annex.
- Trace every non-originating input through the bill of materials, with its classification and value, and keep supplier declarations that support originating status.
- Proof of origin differs by agreement: a certification with prescribed minimum data elements (USMCA), an origin statement on the invoice or the importer's knowledge (EU-UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement), or certificates issued by an authority. Retain the supporting evidence for the agreement's retention period.
- Routing goods through a third country with minimal processing does not change origin, and evading anti-dumping or countervailing duties this way is investigated and penalised.

## Incoterms 2020

Incoterms are International Chamber of Commerce rules for delivery: where risk passes, who arranges and pays for carriage, insurance and clearance. Incoterms 2020 is the current edition as of late 2026; always write the rule, the named place and the edition, for example "FCA Seller's warehouse, 12 Example Road, Rotterdam, Incoterms® 2020".

| Rule | Mode | Risk passes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXW | Any | Goods at the buyer's disposal at the seller's premises, not loaded | Seller has no export clearance duty, yet the buyer often cannot act as exporter; FCA usually fits better |
| FCA | Any | Delivered to the buyer's carrier at the named place | Seller clears export. The 2020 edition lets the parties agree that the buyer's carrier issues an on-board bill of lading to the seller (letters of credit) |
| CPT / CIP | Any | On handover to the first carrier | Seller pays carriage to destination. CIP requires all-risks cover (Institute Cargo Clauses (A)) unless agreed otherwise |
| DAP | Any | Ready for unloading at the named destination | Buyer clears import and pays duties |
| DPU | Any | Unloaded at the named destination | Replaced DAT in 2020 |
| DDP | Any | At destination, cleared for import | Seller must be able to act as importer (registration, import VAT, local agent); often impossible or costly |
| FAS / FOB | Sea and inland waterway | Alongside / on board the vessel at the port of shipment | Not suited to containers handed over at a terminal; use FCA |
| CFR / CIF | Sea and inland waterway | On board at the port of shipment | CIF requires only minimum cover (Clauses (C)) unless agreed otherwise |

Incoterms do not decide transfer of title, payment terms, the contract's governing law, or customs value. They apply to domestic sales only if the contract says so.

## Duties, taxes and relief

- Duty depends on classification, value and origin, plus any trade remedies (anti-dumping, countervailing, safeguard) and additional duties a government imposes. Import VAT or GST and excise are separate from customs duty.
- **Low-value shipments:** rules changed sharply in 2025-26. The US suspended duty-free de minimis treatment for imports from all countries from 29 August 2025; the EU has agreed to end its EUR 150 customs-duty exemption for low-value parcels, with an interim flat duty planned from 2026. Check the current rule before quoting any threshold, and do not split shipments to stay under one.
- **Relief programmes to ask the broker about:** foreign-trade zones (US) and customs warehousing or inward processing (EU and UK) to defer or reduce duty; duty drawback on re-exported goods or inputs (US drawback refunds up to 99 % of eligible duties, claims within five years of import); temporary admission, including ATA Carnets for samples, exhibition goods and professional equipment. Each has conditions, approvals and record-keeping duties.

## Restricted-party screening and export controls

- Screen every party to the transaction (customer, consignee, end user, intermediaries, freight forwarder, bank where possible) against the lists that apply to you, for example the US Consolidated Screening List (which combines OFAC's SDN list, BIS's Entity, Denied Persons, Unverified and Military End User lists and others), the EU consolidated list of financial sanctions, and the UK Sanctions List. Screen again when lists update, not only at onboarding.
- Ownership matters: OFAC treats entities owned 50 % or more, directly or indirectly, by blocked persons as blocked even when they are not listed. Other regimes have their own ownership and control tests, and they have been changing; check the current guidance.
- A hit is resolved by comparing full name, aliases, address, country, identifiers and, for individuals, date of birth, and by documenting why it is or is not a match. Unresolved or true matches go to compliance counsel; the transaction waits.
- Red flags beyond lists: reluctance to state end use, products that do not fit the buyer's business, unusual routing or payment, delivery to a forwarder with no clear end user.
- Export controls are separate from sanctions: check the item's control classification (for example an ECCN under the US Export Administration Regulations, or the EU dual-use list) and whether the destination, end use or end user requires a licence. Military items fall under separate regimes (ITAR in the US); jurisdiction questions go to counsel.

## Documents that must agree

Commercial invoice (parties, full description, quantity, unit and total price, currency, Incoterms rule and place, country of origin, payment terms), packing list (weights, dimensions, marks and piece count matching the transport document), transport document (bill of lading or air waybill), origin proof where claiming preference, and any partner-agency certificates (for example food, chemicals, radio equipment). Mismatches between them are a common cause of holds and examinations. In the US, ocean imports also need the Importer Security Filing before loading.

## When something goes wrong

- A hold or examination: find out quickly whether it is documentary (fixable with papers) or substantive (classification, origin, sanctions, product safety), and involve the broker.
- An error discovered in past entries: most regimes reward voluntary disclosure before an investigation starts (in the US, a prior disclosure under 19 CFR 162.74 limits penalties). Whether and how to disclose is a decision for counsel; prepare the facts, entry numbers and corrected data for them.

## Checks (done when)

- [ ] Every classification has a written rationale (headings considered, notes and GRI applied, rulings consulted) and is marked as analysis pending the importer's or broker's confirmation.
- [ ] Valuation states the method, the additions considered and the freight basis for the importing country.
- [ ] Origin claims trace each non-originating input to the agreement's product-specific rule, with supplier evidence on file.
- [ ] The Incoterms rule, named place and edition are written in full and match the payment and insurance arrangements.
- [ ] Rates, thresholds and lists carry the date they were checked; nothing about low-value shipments is quoted from memory.
- [ ] Screening covers all parties, hits are documented, and nothing proceeds on an unresolved match.
