# Brand discovery and brand voice

Read this when the task is defining or repositioning a brand (purpose, positioning, audience, personality, voice, story), writing a brandbook to brief designers and writers, or capturing a written voice from real samples so drafts sound like the author. Applying a visual identity to slides, documents and pages is `../../creative-media/references/themes-and-brand-application.md`; brand rules for UI are in `../../ui-rules.md` (brand identity category); competitor scoping is `competitive-analysis.md`; keeping an author's voice in long-form writing is `../../research-writing/references/content-and-coauthoring.md`.

## Ground rules

- **The brand's substance comes from the people who run it and from customers**, never from the agent. Elicit, paraphrase back, and let them choose the wording.
- **Use real evidence:** customer quotes, sales conversations, reviews (used for themes, not counts), the founder's own writing. Mark anything inferred.
- **One question at a time** during interviews; lists of questions get checklist answers.
- **Persist answers with consent.** Interview notes and voice profiles are about real people; store them where the user chooses, keep them out of public repositories unless asked, and delete them on request.
- **Never imitate a living person's voice to deceive.** A voice profile helps an author write consistently as themselves or as their organisation; it is not for ghost-writing as someone who has not agreed.

## Discovery interview

Run it over one or several sessions; record raw quotes and your synthesis separately, so the record can be checked later.

| Topic | What to elicit | Useful prompts |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The belief behind the work, separate from what is sold; what the organisation refuses to do | "Why does that matter to you?" repeated until a value surfaces (laddering) |
| Positioning | Target customer, the category they would put you in, competitive alternatives (including doing nothing or doing it in-house), what is unique and why it matters to that customer | "What would they use if you did not exist?" |
| Audience | The best-fit customer as a specific situation: trigger, what they tried, what success means in their words, what they fear; worst-fit customers | "Tell me about the last customer who was a perfect fit" |
| Personality | How the brand behaves: traits with their limits ("direct, never rude") | "If the brand walked into a meeting, how would it act?" |
| Voice | Copy they admire and copy that feels wrong, with reasons; words they always and never use | "Which sentence here sounds like you, and which does not?" |
| Story | The origin and the turning point, told truthfully | "What happened that made you start?" |
| Founder versus company | What the founder's personal brand owns and what the company owns | "What should still be true if you stepped back?" |

Interview discipline: paraphrase each answer and ask one deepening question or close the thread; when an answer is generic, ask for one concrete example, story or number; stop a topic when two probes add nothing new. With several founders, interview them separately first, then reconcile: list agreements, differences and productive tensions for a joint session.

## Synthesis: the brandbook

Keep it short enough to be read before every brief:

1. **Purpose:** one sentence plus three behaviours that show it.
2. **Positioning statement:** "For [target customer] who [situation], [brand] is the [category] that [unique value]. Unlike [alternatives], we [key difference]." Offer two or three versions that vary the category or the difference, and record which the owners chose and why.
3. **Audience portrait:** situation, trigger, desired outcome, objections, disqualifiers.
4. **Personality and voice:** traits with boundaries, voice statement, tone shifts by context.
5. **Story:** a paragraph usable on an About page, true in every detail.
6. **Proof:** the evidence that backs each claim (case studies, numbers with sources).
7. **Open questions and tensions** resolved or deferred, with owners.

Frameworks such as archetypes or identity prisms are optional lenses; include one only if it helps the team decide something.

## Voice profile from real samples

1. **Collect 5 to 20 samples** the author or organisation actually wrote and is happy with: posts, essays, launch notes, emails that worked, docs. Recent material first unless the user says older writing is more representative. Keep public voice and private working voice separate if they differ.
2. **Extract observable features**, each with an example from the samples:

| Feature | Questions |
|---|---|
| Rhythm | Sentence length and variation; fragments; paragraph length |
| Density | Compressed or explanatory; how much context is assumed |
| Claims | How claims are made and backed: numbers, mechanisms, examples, links |
| Structure | How pieces open and close; transitions; lists versus prose |
| Mechanics | Capitalisation, punctuation habits, parentheticals, questions, emoji |
| Vocabulary | Recurring words and phrases; words never used |
| Calls to action | Whether, when and how the author asks for something |
| Channel differences | What changes between a short post, a long article and an email |

3. **Write the profile** as short bullets with a confidence note and the list of samples used. Every "never" rule must be visible in the samples or explicitly requested; do not invent bans from general taste.
4. **Test it:** draft two pieces from the profile and have the author mark what sounds wrong; update the profile, not only the drafts.

```text
VOICE PROFILE  (author or brand, date, confidence)
Samples:       list with dates
Rhythm:        ...
Density:       ...
Claims:        ...
Structure:     ...
Mechanics:     ...
Vocabulary:    uses ... / avoids ...
Calls to act:  ...
Channels:      short post ... / article ... / email ...
```

## Using voice and brand in drafts

- Reuse the confirmed profile across a campaign instead of rebuilding it per piece.
- Specifics beat adjectives: replace "powerful", "seamless", "game-changing" with what the product does and the evidence for it.
- Tone shifts by context (an outage notice is not a launch post), the voice does not.
- Generic AI phrasing to remove on sight: throat-clearing openers ("In today's fast-moving world"), empty pivots ("Here's the thing"), bait questions added only to drive replies, and lists of three adjectives.

## Checks (done when)

- [ ] Brand statements trace to the owners' own words or customer evidence, with raw notes kept separately from synthesis.
- [ ] The positioning statement names a real customer situation, real alternatives and a difference the owners can prove.
- [ ] The voice profile cites the samples it came from and was tested on new drafts with the author's corrections applied.
- [ ] Interview notes and profiles are stored where the user chose, with their consent, and can be deleted.
