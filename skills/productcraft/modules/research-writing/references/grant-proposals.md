# Grant proposals

Read this when the user is planning, drafting, critiquing or checking a research funding proposal: a national agency grant, a foundation or charity application, an industry-funded project or a fellowship. Peer review of proposals uses the same methods as `peer-review.md`; timelines and diagrams are in `diagrams-and-mermaid.md`.

## Ground rules

1. **The call is the specification.** The solicitation, funding opportunity, work programme or call text defines eligibility, sections, page and font limits, required documents, review criteria and deadlines, and these change every cycle. Read the current call in full and build a compliance matrix from it before writing a word. When the call and this file disagree, the call wins.
2. **The science and the text belong to the applicant.** Funders increasingly restrict AI-written applications: NIH announced in 2025 that applications substantially developed by AI will not be treated as the applicant's original work, and capped the number of applications per investigator per year; European Commission guidance on generative AI in research keeps researchers fully responsible for content and asks for transparency about substantial use. Check the funder's current policy with the user. Help with structure, critique, compliance, clarity and editing of the applicant's own drafts; do not ghost-write aims or ideas the applicant has not formed.
3. **Never invent facts:** preliminary results, publication records, institutional resources, letters of support, salaries, rates or costs. Leave `[[APPLICANT: ...]]` placeholders and list them.
4. **The institution submits.** The research office (sponsored programs) checks budgets and signs; involve it early, and do not submit anything yourself.

## Anatomy shared by most proposals

- **Summary or abstract:** the problem, the objective, the approach and the expected outcome, readable by a non-specialist panel member.
- **Aims or objectives:** two to four, each with its hypothesis or question, approach and expected outcome. Avoid aims that only work if the previous one succeeds; if they must be sequential, give the fallback.
- **Significance and state of the art:** the gap, with cited evidence (verified as in `citations-and-reference-managers.md`), and why closing it matters now.
- **Innovation:** what is new in concept, method or application, stated concretely; "novel" is not an argument.
- **Approach:** design, methods, sample sizes with justification, analysis plan, rigor (controls, blinding, randomization, relevant biological variables), preliminary data honestly labeled, expected results, risks with alternatives, and decision points.
- **Timeline and milestones:** a Gantt chart or table with measurable milestones and go/no-go points.
- **Team and environment:** why this team can do it; who does what; resources and access that are already in place.
- **Impact:** for the field and beyond; for funders with an explicit impact criterion, a concrete plan (who benefits, how, with what activities and measures).
- **Data management and sharing plan:** what data, which standards, which repository, when, under which license, with what restrictions (see `study-checklists-and-registration.md`).
- **Budget justification:** every cost tied to an aim or activity.

## Funder notes (current in 2025-2026; verify in the call)

- **NIH research project grants** are reviewed under the simplified framework introduced for due dates from January 2025: Importance of the Research (significance and innovation) and Rigor and Feasibility (approach) are each scored 1 to 9 (1 is best); Expertise and Resources (investigators and environment) is assessed as sufficient or with gaps, not scored; reviewers give an overall impact score. Typical page limits: Specific Aims 1 page; Research Strategy 12 pages for an R01 and 6 for an R21. A Data Management and Sharing Plan has been required since 2023.
- **NSF** reviews every proposal on Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts. The Project Summary is one page with Overview, Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts parts; the Project Description is usually limited to 15 pages. Biosketches and current-and-pending support go through SciENcv. NSF's proposal guide is revised regularly, and 2025 brought substantial policy changes; use the version in force for the deadline.
- **ERC frontier research grants** use a single criterion, scientific excellence, applied to both the project and the principal investigator. Page limits for the extended synopsis, CV and full proposal are set in each year's work programme.
- **Horizon Europe collaborative projects** score Excellence, Impact, and Quality and efficiency of implementation, each from 0 to 5, against published thresholds; the impact section expects a pathway from outputs to outcomes with dissemination and exploitation plans.
- **Programme-style or mission agencies** (defense, energy, innovation agencies) often ask variants of a classic set of questions: what are you trying to do, in plain words; how is it done today and what limits it; what is new and why will it work; who cares; what difference does success make; what are the risks; what will it cost and how long will it take; how will progress be measured at the midpoint and the end. Answer each one explicitly.
- **Foundations and charities:** read their priorities and past awards; many want a short letter of inquiry first.

## The NIH-style one-page aims, as a pattern for any short pitch

1. Opening: the problem and its scale, with one or two cited facts.
2. What is known, and the specific gap that blocks progress.
3. Long-term goal, the objective of this application, the central hypothesis and its basis (preliminary data or literature).
4. Aims, each in two or three sentences: what will be done, how, and what will be learned.
5. Payoff: what will be possible when the aims are achieved.

The page must work when read alone, because some reviewers read little else.

## Writing for reviewers

- Reviewers read many proposals quickly. Put the answer to each criterion where they look for it, under headings that use the funder's words.
- Lead with the main point in each section; use one clear figure early (concept or study design).
- Make success measurable: "reduce assay time from 6 hours to under 1 hour with a coefficient of variation below 10 %" instead of "improve efficiency".
- Name the main risks and the plan if they happen; reviewers trust proposals that show they have thought about failure.
- Keep terminology, aim numbering and numbers identical across summary, aims, approach, timeline and budget.
- Do a cold read before submission: a colleague outside the subfield (or a fresh helper given only the documents and the review criteria) scores the draft and says what they could not find.

## Budget and justification

- Personnel in the funder's units (often person-months per year), equipment with quotes, consumables, travel with purpose, publication and data-sharing costs, subcontracts, and indirect costs at the institution's negotiated rate.
- Every line is justified by an activity in the plan. The research office provides rates, salary bases and fringe rates; never estimate them.

## Before submission

- Page limits checked on the final PDFs, after conversion, with the funder's fonts and margins.
- All required documents present, named and formatted as the submission system expects; run the system's validation (Grants.gov, Research.gov, the EU Funding and Tenders portal, or the funder's portal) days before the deadline, not hours.
- References verified; figures legible when printed; links work and are permitted by the call.
- For a resubmission, a response to previous reviews within the allowed space (NIH gives one page), with changes marked as the call requires.

## Checks (done when)

- [ ] A compliance matrix built from the current call exists and every row is satisfied.
- [ ] Each review criterion maps to a visible part of the proposal.
- [ ] Aims are specific, measurable and not fragile to each other's failure; risks have alternatives.
- [ ] No preliminary data, resources, letters, costs or rates were invented; placeholders are listed.
- [ ] The funder's AI policy was checked with the user, and the applicant authored the scientific content.
- [ ] Final PDFs pass page limits and the submission system's validation.
