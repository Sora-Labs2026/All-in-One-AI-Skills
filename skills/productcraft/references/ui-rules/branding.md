# UI rules: Brand identity

Logo construction, app icons, palettes, voice and consistent application. Rule IDs use the `BRD-` prefix; cite them in change reports.

## BRD-01. Build the mark on a grid and document clear space

**Why:** A documented construction and exclusion zone keeps the mark consistent from billboards to favicons.

- **Do:** Construct the symbol and wordmark from simple geometry on guides for baseline, cap height and shared stroke widths.
- **Do:** Define clear space as a multiple of a mark element, a minimum size, and lockups: symbol plus wordmark, symbol only, and a reversed version for photos and saturated backgrounds.
- **Do:** For marks built on a cell grid, publish display sizes that are whole multiples of the cell count (a 5-cell mark at 60, 80 or 100px) so edges land on whole pixels, and check the smallest size on a 1x screen.
- **Avoid:** Stretching, recoloring or crowding the mark, or placing it on busy photography without a scrim.

## BRD-02. Design app icons for small sizes

**Why:** An icon must stay recognizable beside other icons, on any wallpaper and at tiny sizes.

- **Do:** Center one bold symbol at roughly 55-65% of the icon width on a solid background with strong figure-ground contrast.
- **Do:** Preview it at home-screen and settings sizes beside common icons on light, dark and photo wallpapers, and check monochrome and tinted variants.
- **Avoid:** Full wordmarks, thin strokes or fine detail inside the icon, or gradients that turn muddy when small.

## BRD-03. Document the palette with roles, values and approved pairs

**Why:** A named palette with roles keeps color consistent across print, web and product.

- **Do:** Give each color a name, HEX and RGB values and a role (background, text, accent, secondary), and expose them as design tokens in code.
- **Do:** Record which text-on-color pairs are approved, with their contrast ratios.
- **Do:** When the brand accent misses 4.5:1, approve it only for large text (24px, or 18.66px bold) and non-text marks, and add a slightly darker variant of the same hue for body text and links (for example #4074EA, about 4.3:1 on white, becomes #2E67E8, about 5.0:1).
- **Do:** Prefer a soft near-black over pure black for brand dark (for example #2A2A2A, about 14.4:1 on white).
- **Avoid:** Small white text on mid-tone brand colors, or palette value labels printed in near-invisible tints.

## BRD-04. Apply the identity the same way everywhere

**Why:** Recognition comes from a few rules repeated on every touchpoint.

- **Do:** Reuse the same symbol, palette tokens, typeface and image treatment across the product, website, social profiles, print and merchandise.
- **Do:** Carry a signature product element into marketing mockups so product and brand match.
- **Avoid:** New accent colors, logo variants or image treatments per channel.

## BRD-05. Write tone of voice as positions on scales

**Why:** Placing the voice on a few spectra gives writers a concrete target for interface copy.

- **Do:** Define 3-5 scales (funny to serious, formal to casual, enthusiastic to matter-of-fact) and mark the brand's position on each.
- **Do:** Translate positions into concrete copy rules with examples, such as warm but precise for money and no jokes in payment errors.
- **Avoid:** Voice described only as vague adjectives with no examples.
